O’Keeffe Imaging.

Photogrammetry, conservation, and the possibility of looking more closely.

PHOTOGRAPHY / IMAGING / NEW MEXICO / 2012
PROJECT / CONTEXT
2012
← ALL PROJECTS

An early meeting point between photography, computation, and cultural documentation.

The Georgia O’Keeffe Museum and New Mexico Highlands University collaborated on an imaging project exploring photogrammetry and the documentation of O’Keeffe’s home and furnishings in Abiquiú, New Mexico.

The museum-university collaboration included graduate coursework in stereoscopic photogrammetry led by Professor Megan Jacobs. Project documentation names Mariano Ulibarri among the participating students, alongside Eli Gonzales, Daniela de Angeli, and Joey Montoya.

The research considered how photographic images could become three-dimensional records useful for conservation, interpretation, and public access. It connects directly to an ongoing interest in what happens when the photographic image becomes something people can explore, reconstruct, or interact with.
