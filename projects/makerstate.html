New Mexico Makerstate.

Taking making on the road, one library at a time.

LIBRARIES / LEARNING / NEW MEXICO / 2013–2015 and beyond
PROJECT / CONTEXT
2013–2015 and beyond
← ALL PROJECTS

Public and Tribal libraries became places for electronic experiments, 3D printing, making, and intergenerational learning.

The New Mexico Makerstate Initiative grew from partnerships among Parachute Factory, the New Mexico State Library, New Mexico Highlands University, and other collaborators. Its goal was to bring technology-rich, participatory programming into public and Tribal libraries around the state.

Workshops combined accessible introductions to circuits, programming, electronic textiles, and 3D printing with existing local traditions of learning by making. The format was mobile: the work traveled to communities rather than depending on people being able to visit a dedicated makerspace.

The Institute of Museum and Library Services documented a tour of 25 community libraries. A September 2015 account in D-Lib Magazine reported that the broader initiative had reached 42 communities and more than 3,000 people at that point. Those figures describe different reporting snapshots, not a single event.

Mariano was one contributor to a larger effort that included state librarians, media-arts students, AmeriCorps interns, educators, and community hosts.

ROLE / ATTRIBUTION

Parachute Factory founder and program collaborator; maker education and outreach.
