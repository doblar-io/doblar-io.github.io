Parachute Factory.

A community makerspace in Las Vegas, New Mexico.

COMMUNITY / MAKING / LAS VEGAS, NM / 2012 onward
PROJECT / CONTEXT
2012 onward
← ALL PROJECTS

What began as a graduate project became a place for people to experiment with tools, art, technology, and one another.

In 2012, Mariano Ulibarri founded Parachute Factory in Las Vegas, New Mexico, while completing graduate work in media arts at New Mexico Highlands University. The project brought makerspace practices into a rural community with a long history of hands-on skills and artistic traditions.

The space offered access to tools and shared learning, from pinhole photography and simple electronics to digital fabrication. Its public programs emphasized experimentation across generations rather than expertise as a prerequisite.

Parachute Factory later provided a base for the New Mexico Makerstate Initiative, and took part in Harvard Graduate School of Education’s Agency by Design research network. The work was always collaborative: local participants, students, librarians, educators, and community partners were part of making it happen.

ROLE / ATTRIBUTION

Founder and director; community programming and collaborative development.
